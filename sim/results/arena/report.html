Combat Arena Results

Sixteen level-5 fighters (four armor classes × two-handed, sword & shield, grappler and archery; magic users are left out for now) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. This round changes arrows against armor: armor (textile most) stops arrows better than a thrust, and archers carry broadheads (big bleeding wounds, poor against metal) and bodkins (armor-piercing, smaller wounds).

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows, winds up and draws, shoves to regain distance, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules.

For the designer

Where the rules need work

This roundOpposed parries and dodges; a rapier and parrying dagger

  • Parry and dodge are opposed rolls: defender d10 + Training + ⌊(DEX + SPD)/4⌋ against attacker d10 + ⌊(STR + SPD)/4⌋ + the motion's penalty; a heavier attacking weapon is harder to parry. Ties go to the defender. A power blow can't be parried.
  • Rapier and dagger (new style): the off-hand parrying dagger stabs and parries thrusts only. It wins 60% in close quarters and 47% in the open. Against sword and shield it wins 76% in close quarters but 40% in the open.
  • The field now has five styles, so every average shifted. Archers fell to 45% / 29%.

EarlierReady a thrust: the point holds a foe off

  • Ready a thrust (its AP now, its stamina only if made): a foe who steps closer into its reach that pass meets it first, and a hit stops them. Fend off: a long weapon shoves at range 2. A foe shoved back can't make their move that pass.
  • Dagger swaps fell in sword duels: 240 → 65 fights of 320. Fighters stepped onto a readied point 310 times and were stopped 131 times.
  • Close quarters favors reach now: two-handers 47 → 58%, sword and shield 66 → 62%, grapplers 45 → 41%. The open field barely moved.
  • The AI readies only against a move already declared this pass. Speculative readies made mirror matches stand off until they timed out, because whoever acts later just waits. So acting later is the advantage.

EarlierWeapons from physics: deep thrusts kill, daggers wound

  • Damage, speed and STR now come from each weapon's mass, length and point, edge or head, and a wound roll (d10) can make a blow vital. Armor guards the vital spots.
  • One blow against an unarmored CON-9 man: a dagger stab kills 10% of the time (it used to be fatal untended every time); a longsword or spear thrust kills 20–30% at once and is fatal untended (STR 5). Mail and plate make vital wounds rare.
  • Styles, open field / close quarters: two-handers 45 → 48% / 37 → 47%; sword and shield 57 → 61% / 61 → 66%; grapplers 44 → 33% / 60 → 45%; archers 49 → 49% / 36 → 32%.
  • Grapplers lose most: their dagger now does 3 instead of 5, and STR adds little to a point. Letting a pin open the armor's guard on the vitals changed almost nothing.
  • The AI still steps inside a sword's reach with a dagger. Once there, the sword lands only a half-force blunt jab. This is a reach and positioning question, not a damage one.

EarlierArrowheads: archers gain a little, mostly through bleeding

  • Archers average 49% in the open (was 46) and 36% in close quarters (was 31). Other styles moved 2 points or less.
  • Against plate wearers archers win 15% in the open (was 9): bodkins find the gaps a little more often. Plate is still close to arrow-proof.
  • Against mail: no change. A bodkin against mail does what a plain arrow used to.
  • Against leather: archers gained most (close quarters 20% → 36%), almost all from the broadhead's 1.5× bleeding. Leather two-handers are hit hardest. If that's too much, the bleed multiplier is the knob.

DecidedPlate is meant to dominate

Heavy armor wins 76% in the open and 71% in close quarters against the old field. That's accepted: plate's rarity is its balance.

ContextOne-on-one is only part of the picture

These are duels. Group fights (many against one, a monster against heroes, mixed parties) may favor styles that look weak here: archers behind a shield line, mages with time to prepare, two-handers hitting whatever a shield wall holds. A group arena is the natural next test.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Ground matters: two-handers and heavy armor are strongest in close quarters; archers and the unarmored are strongest in the open.
  • The wind-up and draw read as intended: exposed, telegraphed, and decisive when they land.
  • Heavy armor still leads in close quarters (64%).
  • Fights last 5.9 rounds in the open and 4.3 in close quarters.
