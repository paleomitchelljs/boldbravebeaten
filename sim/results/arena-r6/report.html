Combat Arena Results

Twelve level-5 fighters (four armor classes × three fighting styles; magic users are left out for now) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. This round brings back partial damage inside a long weapon's reach, now landing as blunt at half force; makes drawing a weapon cheap and dropping one free; and makes anyone held in a grapple fight cramped, dagger included.

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows, winds up and draws, shoves to regain distance, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules.

  • Sword & shield lost its edge where it used to step inside reach with a dagger: light sword & shield fell from 86% to 74% in close quarters, unarmored from 62% to 35%. Two-handers gained in both arenas.
  • Cramped blows are rare (about 0.1 a fight). The AI still prefers to drop its long weapon and draw the dagger (0.3–0.95 times a fight), now that drawing costs 1 AP.
  • Armor now decides more than style. Every plate fighter wins 64–87%; every unarmored one wins 11–35% and dies 64–89% of the time.
  • Fights last about 3.3 rounds in the open and 3.1 in close quarters.

DecisionIs plate too strong now?

If heavy armor should be strong but not around 80%, the levers are: how much force a cramped blow keeps (now half), whether a cramped blow keeps some of its piercing instead of becoming all blunt, plate's blunt mitigation, and the plate retune from round 3. Grappling rules, still being designed, are the other natural counter to plate.

ContextOne-on-one is only part of the picture

These are duels. Group fights (many against one, a monster against heroes, mixed parties) may favor styles that look weak here: archers behind a shield line, mages with time to prepare, two-handers hitting whatever a shield wall holds. A group arena is the natural next test.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Ground matters: two-handers and heavy armor are strongest in close quarters; archers and the unarmored are strongest in the open.
  • The wind-up and draw read as intended: exposed, telegraphed, and decisive when they land.
  • Heavy armor still leads in close quarters (64%).
  • Fights last 5.9 rounds in the open and 4.3 in close quarters.
