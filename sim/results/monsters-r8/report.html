Monster Arena Trials

Draft creatures (animals, monsters built to beat one kind of fighter, tireless undead and constructs, scaled and slippery humanoids, and a boss) fought every level-5 arena fighter one-on-one in an open field and in close quarters, then fought parties of cooperating adventurers alone and in groups.

Verdicts

Did each monster beat what it was built to beat?

Fighters differ a lot on their own (plate wins about 70% against people, the unarmored about 34%), so raw win rates mislead. The edge is how much better the monster does against its target than its own average, compared with how much better the other monsters do against the same target. Positive means the design works.

What the trials say

Biggest findingGroups, not single monsters, threaten a party

Every single creature loses to a party of cooperating adventurers, even ones that win 70–87% one-on-one. Groups are another story. Against the light party of three, a kobold band (four spears) wins 87–90% of fights, a zombie horde 100%, a froglok patrol 72–90%, a skeleton squad 72–85%. The four veterans beat every group, but the froglok patrol in the open still wins 55% against them and the kobolds kill a veteran a fight. A boss needs its actions multiplied (the troll chieftain at ×2.5) to matter alone.

Undead and constructsTireless creatures play differently

  • Skeletons take no piercing and half slashing, so archers (7–12% win against one) and dagger-wielding grapplers (0%) are helpless. Two-handers, whose greatswords hit hard enough to break bones, win 65–95%. A squad of three beats the light party.
  • Stone golem loses only to the two-handers (76–90% win against it); stone shrugs off everything else.
  • Zombies are slow but never tire; archers lose 68–95% against one because piercing is halved. A horde of four overwhelms a light party.
  • With no stamina, these creatures act on every AP every round and only stop when their body is destroyed. Blunt damage goes straight to the body.

HumanoidsScales, slick skin, packs

  • Kobolds and goblins are weak alone (8–19% one-on-one) and dangerous in bands, kobolds especially: four spears at reach 2–4 with a pack bonus.
  • Frogloks are the strongest of the small humanoids: spear and shield, leaping into the fight, and slippery (+2 against being grappled; grapplers win 37–43% against one).
  • Sarnak: scaled, warhammer and tail sweep; 63–70% one-on-one, hardest on the lightly armored.

Odd propertiesChitin, webs, and the displacer

  • Springy chitin (spider, rust beetle): each blow that lands costs the attacker 3 stamina.
  • Sticky webs made the spider much stronger (61–62% one-on-one, and 35% even against plate, where it had 7%). Each layer adds 2 AP and 3 stamina to every action; one layer wears off each round. Before the wear-off it won 68–82%.
  • Displacer beast rolls to be found: arrows hit only an illusion on 1–5 of a d10, blows on 1–2. Even a pair rarely threatens a party, because the party's front line handles them while the archer is mostly wasted.

Earlier countersStill standing

  • Rust beetle still beats plate best of all the monsters; owlbear still wrecks two-handers and archers in close quarters; the cave troll beats everyone and reads as a boss.
