Combat Arena Results

Sixteen level-5 fighters (four armor classes × four fighting styles: two-handed, sword & shield, archery, and the new grappler; magic users are left out for now) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. This round adds the grappling ladder (clinch → hold → pin, with throws and breaks), each step a directly opposed roll; plate is easy to grab but hard to throw.

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows, winds up and draws, shoves to regain distance, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules.

For the designer

Where the rules need work

This roundGrapplers: strong in close quarters, held off in the open

  • Grapplers average 45% in the open and 61% in close quarters. In close quarters they beat two-handers 69% and archers 80%, but only 43% against sword & shield.
  • By opponent armor (close / open): plate 48 / 33, mail 70 / 45, leather 63 / 45, unarmored 74 / 53. Plate's handles help, but plate fighters hit hard enough to win the race.
  • The ladder in use (per fight with a grappler): 0.43 clinches, 0.49 straight to a hold on a big win, 0.34 pins, 1.3 dagger stabs, 0.30 successful breaks. The upkeep bites: 0.23 times a fight a holder is too spent to keep the hold.
  • Throws never happen: the AI pins and stabs instead, so a throw into plate's whole-round stand-up is untested.
  • The other styles barely moved (within 2 points) against the opponents they already had.

DecidedPlate is meant to dominate

Heavy armor wins 76% in the open and 71% in close quarters against the old field. That's accepted: plate's rarity is its balance.

ContextOne-on-one is only part of the picture

These are duels. Group fights (many against one, a monster against heroes, mixed parties) may favor styles that look weak here: archers behind a shield line, mages with time to prepare, two-handers hitting whatever a shield wall holds. A group arena is the natural next test.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Ground matters: two-handers and heavy armor are strongest in close quarters; archers and the unarmored are strongest in the open.
  • The wind-up and draw read as intended: exposed, telegraphed, and decisive when they land.
  • Heavy armor still leads in close quarters (64%).
  • Fights last 5.9 rounds in the open and 4.3 in close quarters.
