Combat Arena Results

Sixteen level-5 fighters (four armor classes × four fighting styles) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. This round replaces the defense step with guards: on your own turn you ready a block, parry or dodge for the next attack that lands on you, or the next from a foe you name. Each pass still resolves at its end, under a 4-AP cap per pass.

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows and spells, winds up and draws, shoves to regain distance, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules.

Spread is the gap between the best and worst of the three weapon styles, in points.

  • Guards favor sword & shield (+5 points in the open, +6 in close quarters). A buckler or round-shield block is cheap enough to ready blind and still attack in the same turn; a parry or dodge costs a whole pass. Defenses were used about 40% more often and hits fell about 10%.
  • How long a guard lasts made no difference with this AI, which readies guards when an attack is coming this pass and so almost always uses them in that pass.
  • A 6-AP cap with guards is the most even of every version run so far (spread 19 / 23): two-handers recover to 46% in the open and archers to 50% in close quarters. Magic drops to 23 / 19.

DecisionSword & shield still dominates; nothing keeps the dagger out

Sword & shield is at 68% in the open and 78% in close quarters with guards. Shove is still used about 0.03 times a fight. Resolving each pass doesn't give a reach weapon a way to stop someone walking in: the step and the attack still happen in the same pass, and nobody reacts to it. Keeping someone at range needs a rule that reacts to the approach.

Options, unchanged: a set or stop-thrust (a ready reach weapon strikes once at anyone entering its band); extra AP to step inside an enemy's reach; a shove that also costs the target AP; no dagger with a shield.

DeferredMagic is still weak

Casters average 31% in the open and 23% in close quarters. The magic stub is unchanged and set aside, as agreed.

ContextOne-on-one is only part of the picture

These are duels. Group fights (many against one, a monster against heroes, mixed parties) may favor styles that look weak here: archers behind a shield line, mages with time to prepare, two-handers hitting whatever a shield wall holds. A group arena is the natural next test.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Ground matters: two-handers and heavy armor are strongest in close quarters; archers and the unarmored are strongest in the open.
  • The wind-up and draw read as intended: exposed, telegraphed, and decisive when they land.
  • Heavy armor still leads in close quarters (64%).
  • Fights last 5.9 rounds in the open and 4.3 in close quarters.
