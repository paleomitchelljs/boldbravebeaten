Combat Arena Results

Sixteen level-5 fighters (four armor classes × four fighting styles) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. The tables show who won, how fights ended, and what the new range, wind-up, archery and magic rules actually did.

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows and spells, winds up power swings, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules written for this test.

For the designer

Where the rules need work

RulesA shield stops anything, and the dagger does the work

Sword and shield is the best melee style (light: 79% open, 90% close). Two-handers win only 25% of their fights against it in the open and 14% in close quarters. Shield users block 1–3 blows a fight and draw their dagger 1.1–1.5 times a fight. Two things combine:

  • A successful block negates the whole blow, however big. A power swing is telegraphed by its wind-up, so the shield user knows exactly which strike to block.
  • Most weapons do half damage at 1 square; the dagger doesn't. Shield plus dagger at clinch distance beats everything that has to stand off.

Options: let heavy blows push through a block (damage above the shield's break threshold reaches the bearer as blunt); make blocking a power swing cost more AP; soften the up-close penalty (×0.75); make drawing a sidearm slower than 4 AP.

NumbersHeavy armor protects, then exhausts

Plate fighters almost never die (0–0.1 deaths per fight) but lose by collapsing (0.4–0.6 per fight). Heavy armor averages 44% in the open and 49% in close quarters; light armor 58% and 54%. "Armor is king in melee" isn't true here.

  • In plate a thrust costs 6 stamina against 4 in leather, and plate regenerates −1 to −2 a round. Attacking into a shield burns a plate fighter out in about five rounds.
  • The ×3 stamina cost came from round-one tuning, before ranges and wind-ups existed. The round-one sims found ×3 fixed fatigue and armored mirrors; this arena suggests it is now too harsh on plate.
  • Plate Mobility II needs a second Armor-tree feat first, so level-5 plate wearers carry −2 SPD and have 8 AP to everyone else's 10.

Options: retest stamina at ×2; cut plate's per-action stamina penalty or its regeneration penalty; add a novice plate feat so Mobility II isn't a tax.

RulesOne arrow decides an open-field fight

Archers win 63% in the open (78% against two-handers) and about 50% in close quarters. They fire only about one arrow a fight, because a longbow shot costs 8 of 12 AP and 8–10 stamina. But arrows never miss: only a dodge or block stops them. A longbow hit puts 7 piercing into an unarmored target, which kills it.

  • The open-versus-confined swing is what your Philosophy wants: terrain as a balancing tool.
  • The problem is that one unanswered shot is fatal, and DEX does nothing for an archer.

Options: an accuracy roll by range and DEX; arrows lose damage at range or against moving targets; cheaper, weaker shots. Bow numbers are all placeholders.

NumbersMagic is weak and risky, not "powerful"

Casters average 32% in the open and 25% in close quarters. A level-5 mage has 14 spell points, enough for 1–2 spells a fight. Backlash hits on about 40% of casts (0.5–0.7 per fight). A Launch + Fire bolt does about 1 CON to someone in leather.

  • Your Philosophy says magic is powerful and needs preparation or carries risk. This stub has the cost and risk without the power.
  • Heavy mages do best in close quarters (43%) only because their plate keeps them alive.

Options: larger word magnitudes; fire that ignores more armor; backlash only on a natural failure; a few spell points regained each round. Every magic number is a placeholder.

RulesThe wind-up works, but power swings rarely land

Two-handers wind up 0.3–1 times a fight but land only 0.1–0.6 power swings. Wind-ups are almost never lost; the release is simply blocked, dodged, or outpaced. Two-handers average 36% in the open and 42% in close quarters. Unarmored two-handers are the weakest fighters in the arena (18% open).

  • The mechanism does what you described: the swinger is exposed, and the blow comes a round later.
  • Because every declaration is visible, though, the target always knows it's coming.

Options: let Feint hide a wind-up; make a power swing unblockable by bucklers and round shields; let power swings partially pass a block (see the first finding).

DecisionTwo magic feat trees could never advance

As transcribed, Casting and Fire Affinity can never pass rank I. Each tree's only novice feat is rank I, and everything after it needs two feats already in that tree. Your Specifics calls magic "four interlocking sub-trees", so I now count tiers across all four. That made every feat reachable, and a test keeps it that way. Please confirm that reading.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Fights are short: 2–5 rounds on average for every matchup.
  • Ground matters. Archers drop from 63% in the open to 50% in close quarters, and shield fighters rise from 58% to 72%.
  • Range bands change behavior: fighters step to their weapon's best distance, and up-close penalties are dodged by swapping weapons rather than eaten.
  • Unarmored fighters lose in melee but win at range (unarmored archers 72% in the open). That's your "speed wins elsewhere."
