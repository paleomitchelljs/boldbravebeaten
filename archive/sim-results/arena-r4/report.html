Combat Arena Results

Sixteen level-5 fighters (four armor classes × four fighting styles) fought every other fighter in an open field and in close quarters. This round changes how a round runs: each pass now resolves at its end, and a character may spend at most 4 AP per pass (2 base AP) as a trial. Anything costing more lands in a later pass.

Every fight is played by the built-in AI. It keeps its preferred range, kites with bows and spells, winds up and draws, shoves to regain distance, swaps between weapon and dagger, and defends by the odds, but it never retreats to heal, flanks, or plans across rounds. Read these as what the rules produce under competent, simple play. Bows and magic are placeholder rules.

  • Resolving each pass on its own changes little. It doesn't stop sword & shield; in close quarters it gains a few points.
  • The 4-AP cap mostly hurts two-handers (−9 in the open against no cap): a 7–8 AP two-handed swing now spans two passes and lands late, where the foe can see it coming. Drawing a sidearm went up by half, because cheap dagger thrusts fit inside the cap.
  • Moving and drawing a bow can no longer share a pass (the draw alone uses 4 AP). Archers lost 3–4 points in the open.
  • A 6-AP cap gives the tightest spread in the open (17 points across the three weapon styles), but close quarters doesn't change.

DecisionSword & shield still dominates; resolving each pass didn't keep the dagger out

Sword & shield is at 63% in the open and 72% in close quarters under the cap. Shove is still used about 0.05 times a fight. Resolving each pass doesn't give a reach weapon a way to stop someone walking in: the step and the attack still happen in the same pass, and nobody reacts to it. Keeping someone at range needs a rule that reacts to the approach.

Options, unchanged: a set or stop-thrust (a ready reach weapon strikes once at anyone entering its band); extra AP to step inside an enemy's reach; a shove that also costs the target AP; no dagger with a shield.

DeferredMagic is still weak

Casters average 33% in the open and 24% in close quarters, up from 23% and 17% because foes' swings now land later. The magic stub is unchanged and set aside, as agreed.

ContextOne-on-one is only part of the picture

These are duels. Group fights (many against one, a monster against heroes, mixed parties) may favor styles that look weak here: archers behind a shield line, mages with time to prepare, two-handers hitting whatever a shield wall holds. A group arena is the natural next test.

WorkingWhat looks right

  • Ground matters: two-handers and heavy armor are strongest in close quarters; archers and the unarmored are strongest in the open.
  • The wind-up and draw read as intended: exposed, telegraphed, and decisive when they land.
  • Armor ordering in melee is closer to your design: heavy armor leads in close quarters (70% under the cap).
  • Fights are a little shorter than round 3 (5.6 and 3.8 rounds).
